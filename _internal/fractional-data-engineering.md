# Fractional Data Engineering — Internal Offering Guide

**Status:** Draft for internal review. Pricing and contract terms require approval.
**Owner:** EmptyCup Consulting
**Public landing page:** `/services/fractional-data-engineering/`

## Positioning

Fractional engineering delivery for SMBs and IT/data leaders with an active backlog but insufficient full-time engineering capacity. Core promise: experienced engineering, predictable fixed sprint fees, and flexible three-week commitments. Differentiate from staff augmentation through planned delivery, engineering standards, backlog visibility, architecture oversight, and knowledge transfer.

## Delivery model

- **Sprint:** 3 weeks; one-sprint minimum commitment; fixed fee for specified reserved capacity.
- **Product increment (PI):** Four consecutive sprints (12 weeks), with a shared objective and rolling roadmap. **Planning horizon, not a 12-week purchase commitment.**
- **Renewal:** Client can renew, change capacity subject to availability, or stop at each sprint boundary.
- **Backlog:** Client controls business priorities; EmptyCup estimates and coordinates engineering. Agree on sprint goals together.
- **Scope:** Fixed fee buys reserved capacity, not guaranteed completion of a fixed backlog or story count.
- **PI review:** Assess integrated capabilities, business value, risks, and next priorities. If engagement ends early, perform a proportionate handoff.

**Open pricing decisions:** Approval authority for rate deviations; additional capacity block sizes; whether onboarding is included; treatment of holidays, delayed access, and client-caused blockers.

## Sprint operating model

**Before the sprint:** Confirm client contact, availability, fee, reserved hours, access, dependencies, sprint objective, prioritized backlog, and acceptance criteria.

**Week 1 — Plan and start:** Planning session, estimates, risk identification, highest-priority work.

**Week 2 — Deliver and adjust:** Engineering, tests, reviews, documentation, progress and blocker updates. Reprioritize when assumptions change.

**Week 3 — Complete and review:** Finish feasible work, demonstrate results, identify incomplete work and technical debt, refresh the backlog and 12-week forecast, discuss renewal.

**Definition of Done, adapted per client:** Work committed to agreed source control; appropriate testing and code review; documentation updated; deployment completed where in scope and permitted; unresolved risks and follow-ups recorded. Do not claim deployment is complete if client approval blocks it.

**Reporting:** Lightweight weekly written update on completed/in-progress work, blockers, capacity consumed and forecast, and decisions needed. Demonstrate concrete evidence of progress at sprint review.

| Production incidents / after-hours | Excluded unless separately contracted |

| IP | Define client rights in delivered work while protecting pre-existing and reusable EmptyCup IP |

Avoid promising specific outcomes, uninterrupted staffing, SLAs, fixed story counts, or completion dates independent of actual capacity and dependencies. Counsel should review binding terms.

## Service boundaries

**Typical work:** Ingestion/CDC, ETL/ELT, orchestration, transformations, dimensional modeling, data quality, monitoring, CI/CD, warehouse tuning, modernization, troubleshooting, technical debt, and documentation.

**Excluded unless separately scoped:** 24/7 operations, on-call support, guaranteed incident response, software licenses and cloud costs, major discovery, unlimited revisions, out-of-scope platform administration, and third-party vendor obligations.

**Essential tier caution:** Thirty hours across three weeks is best for well-defined enhancements and maintenance. Meeting overhead can materially reduce delivery time; avoid positioning this tier for complex greenfield work.

## Qualification and onboarding

Discovery covers business objective, current platform and architecture, backlog maturity, technical ownership, repository and deployment access, security, dependencies, decision-maker, capacity, and first sprint goal. Offer a separately scoped assessment where uncertainty makes responsible planning impossible; assessments are not mandatory.
